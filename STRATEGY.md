# Mind the Gap: audience & evergreen content strategy

## 1. Reality check: "viral forever" vs. evergreen
- **Viral** is a feed event: the first days decide it, and luck is a big part. Nobody can guarantee it.
- Shorts views usually **peak within about a month**; what keeps a Short earning for years is **search**
  (people typing a problem into YouTube/Google) and a growing back-catalogue that new viewers binge.
- So we engineer what *can* be engineered: **evergreen topics** (never date), **search-phrased titles**,
  and **retention mechanics** (hook, open loop, payoff, loop) that make the algorithm keep re-testing a video.

## 2. Who we make this for
Shorts skew young: roughly half of viewers are 18-34 (aggregator estimates), slightly male-leaning. Inside the
stated 22-45 UK/US range, the core viewer is narrower:

**The "stuck striver", 21-34.** Early career or a few years in. Ambitious but inconsistent. Phone-heavy,
money-tight, tired. Has tried productivity tips and guru content and is sceptical of both. Feels behind
compared with friends online. Wants to fix *one* thing without a lifestyle overhaul.

What they search, in their own words:
- "why am I always tired"
- "why can't I stop scrolling"
- "why am I always broke"
- "how to stop procrastinating"
- "why do I overthink"
- "why can't I stay motivated"

**Secondary: 35-45 resetters.** Same problems, more money and family at stake: habits, money, burnout,
"starting over". The same videos serve them when the stakes are universal (time, money, energy).

Gender balance: stories use neutral characters and everyday stakes (phone, money, sleep, work, gym, plans),
never gendered stereotypes.

## 3. The evergreen rules (every video)
1. **A daily-life trigger.** Something the viewer did today: unlocking the phone, a "50% off" banner,
   "I'll start Monday", a 2am scroll.
2. **A real, well-replicated effect** named once ("THAT'S ANCHORING."). No replication-crisis favourites
   (ego depletion, power posing, most social priming, "10% of your brain", left/right brain).
3. **No dates, prices that age or trends.** Use relative money ($300/mo spare), no years, no app names that
   can die, no memes.
4. **A search-phrased title, 5 words max**, as the question the viewer would type: "Why You Can't Stop
   Checking", "Why Monday Never Comes".
5. **The mirror cast** (see CHANNEL.md): A = what most people do, B = the extreme, winner = A + one change.
6. **Open loop from frame 1:** a gold question at the top ("WHO KEEPS THE MOST MONEY?"), answered on screen
   at the climax ("IT'S C: OWN PRICE"). Viewers silently pick A/B/C in beat 1 (badges shown), then stay to
   see if they were right.
7. **Payoff + identity ending:** "WHICH ONE ARE YOU? COMMENT A · B · C" drives comments; the loop/rewind
   ending drives rewatches.
8. **Still camera, moving text:** characters carry the story; captions, numbers and the open loop carry
   the emphasis.

## 6. Publishing system
- **1-5 Shorts a week** (consistent day/time). Each is reviewed by the two-layer review in README.
- **Title** = the search question. **Description**: first line restates the problem in plain words, then
  the effect name, then "(Illustrative numbers.)". 3-5 hashtags: #psychology #mindset + topic.
- **Pinned comment**: "A, B or C? Be honest." to seed comments.
- **Playlists by pillar** (Money brain, Phone brain, Motivation brain, Social brain) for binge sessions.
- **Every 8-10 Shorts: a long-form compilation** ("8 brain tricks that keep you broke"). Long-form keeps
  earning from search for years, which Shorts alone usually don't.

## 7. What to measure (YouTube Studio)
| Metric | Target | Lever |
|---|---|---|
| Viewed vs. swiped away | above channel median | first caption plus the open-loop question |
| Average % viewed | 80% or more (looping above 100% is the goal) | pacing, the loop ending |
| Comments per 1k views | rising | the A/B/C ask, relatability of option A |
| Search traffic share after 30 days | growing | search-phrased titles |
| Subscribers per 1k views | rising | end card, series consistency |

Kill a topic format after 3 weak videos; double down on formats whose viewed-vs-swiped beats the median.
